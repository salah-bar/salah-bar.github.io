Adhan on Google Nest speakers with Home Assistant

Salah Bar can play the adhan on your Google Nest and Google Home speakers at the exact times the app shows, even when your phone and computer are away from home, through your own Home Assistant.

Why Home Assistant?

Google doesn’t let apps play their own sound on Nest speakers from the internet. A Google Home routine can only speak text or ask the assistant to play something from a music service, so it can’t play Salah Bar’s adhan at the right second. Something at home has to send the sound to the speaker:

  • Salah Bar for Mac or Windows does it over your Wi-Fi when that computer is on and at home (Settings → Smart Home → Speakers). Nothing else is needed.
  • Home Assistant does it when no Salah Bar computer is at home. This page is about that.

How it works: the Salah Bar app sends your home’s adhan times for the next year to Salah Bar’s server. The server calculates nothing; it only keeps the times the app worked out. About 20 seconds before each adhan, the server calls your Home Assistant’s webhook with the exact time and the address of the adhan recording. Home Assistant waits until that second on its own clock and plays the adhan on your speakers.

What you need

  • Home Assistant running at home, for example a Home Assistant Green or a Raspberry Pi, with the Google Cast integration. It finds your Nest speakers by itself.
  • A way for the internet to reach one webhook: Home Assistant Cloud (Nabu Casa, a paid subscription with a free trial), or your own https address for Home Assistant.
  • Salah Bar for Mac, iPhone or Android, to set it up and to send the times. It only has to run now and then (the times last a year), not at prayer time.

Set it up

  1. In Home Assistant, make an automation. Settings → Automations & scenes → Create automation → Create new automation → ⋮ → Edit in YAML. Paste the automation below (Salah Bar’s Copy automation button copies the same text). Replace media_player.YOUR_SPEAKER with your speaker, or a speaker group, from Settings → Devices & services → Google Cast.
  2. Make the webhook reachable. Switch to the visual editor, open the Webhook trigger’s settings (the gear), turn off “Only accessible from the local network”, keep only POST, and save. Home Assistant made a webhook ID that’s hard to guess; keep it secret, like a password.
  3. Copy its address.
    • With Home Assistant Cloud: Settings → Home Assistant Cloud → Webhooks → turn on the switch next to “Salah Bar adhan” and copy the public address (https://hooks.nabu.casa/…).
    • With your own address: https://your-home-assistant-address/api/webhook/<the webhook ID> (port 8123 is fine).
  4. In Salah Bar: Settings → Smart Home → Google Home (via Home Assistant) → paste the address → Connect. Choose the prayers.
  5. Press Test. About 20 seconds later your speaker should start the adhan. If it starts a little late, raise Start early by (most speakers need about a second to load the sound).
alias: Salah Bar adhan
mode: parallel
triggers:
  - trigger: webhook
    webhook_id: "PASTE-YOUR-WEBHOOK-ID"
    allowed_methods: [POST]
    local_only: false
conditions:
  - condition: template
    value_template: "{{ trigger.json.app == 'salah-bar' and trigger.json.event == 'adhan_due' }}"
actions:
  - delay:
      seconds: "{{ [trigger.json.epoch - as_timestamp(now()) - (trigger.json.lead | float(0)), 0] | max }}"
  - action: media_player.play_media
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.YOUR_SPEAKER
    data:
      media_content_id: "{{ trigger.json.url }}"
      media_content_type: music

More than one speaker, or different speakers per prayer

  • Several speakers at once: list them under entity_id, or use a speaker group made in the Google Home app.
  • Different speakers per prayer: replace the last action with a choose on trigger.json.prayer (fajr, dhuhr, asr, maghrib, isha). For example, Fajr only in the bedroom.
  • A set volume for the adhan: add a media_player.volume_set action before play_media.

What Salah Bar sends to your Home Assistant

{"app": "salah-bar", "event": "adhan_due", "prayer": "maghrib",
 "time": "2026-11-26T14:52:00Z", "epoch": 1795704720, "lead": 1.0,
 "url": "https://…/audio/adhan-madinah.m4a", "test": false}

time and epoch are the adhan’s exact time; lead is your “start early by” in seconds; url is the recording (Fajr has its own); test is true for the Test button. Nothing else: no city, no location.

Good to know

  • Check with Test. Home Assistant answers “OK” even to a webhook it doesn’t know, so the app can only show that a call was sent, not that the speaker played. Test is how you know it works.
  • It needs your home’s internet and Home Assistant. If either is down at prayer time, that adhan doesn’t play.
  • What was playing stops. Home Assistant can’t put back music that was playing on a Nest before the adhan.
  • The adhan recording is one of Salah Bar’s own, the same one Amazon Echo plays. A recording you added to the app yourself isn’t on the server, so it can’t be played this way.
  • Turn it off in Salah Bar (Remove). The server deletes your webhook address at once. Unlinking deletes everything the server keeps for you.
  • Privacy: the server keeps your webhook address encrypted and shows the app only its host name. A Home Assistant Cloud address (hooks.nabu.casa) reveals nothing about you. See the privacy policy.
