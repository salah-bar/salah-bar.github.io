Salah Bar Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

English · Türkçe · العربية · اردو · Bahasa Indonesia · Bahasa Melayu · Français · Русский · Deutsch · Español · فارسی · বাংলা

In short: Salah Bar has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and the developer never receives any of your data, except the adhan times you send to Salah Bar’s server if you link an Amazon Echo (details). Your settings, your prayer log and the rest of your data stay on your own devices. Features you choose send only what they need to the service that answers: the weather and Nearby mosques send your location, rounded, to the weather or map service; maps load images of the area shown; downloads ask for the files you pick. Each app’s policy below lists every one of them.

This page covers every Salah Bar app. Each app’s full policy, with everything it does on the device and every service it can connect to, is here:

  • Salah Bar for Mac, iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch
  • Salah Bar for Android and Wear OS
  • Salah Bar for Windows

The section below, on signing in with Google, applies to all of them.

Sync with Google Drive: what Salah Bar does with your Google data

Sync with Google Drive is optional, and off until you tap Sign in with Google in Salah Bar’s sync settings. It keeps your Salah Bar data the same on all your devices.

What Salah Bar asks Google for

  • One permission only: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata, which Google shows as “See, create and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive”. It lets Salah Bar keep its own file in a hidden app folder of your Drive. Salah Bar can’t see, open, change or delete any other file in your Drive.
  • Your Google account’s email address, only to show which account is signed in.

Salah Bar asks for nothing else: not your name, contacts, photos, calendar or other files.

What is stored, and where

  • In your Google Drive, in the hidden app folder (it doesn’t appear in your list of files): one file, Salah Bar sync.json. For each of your devices it holds the device’s name and platform and your Salah Bar data: settings and saved cities, prayer log, fasts, Quran progress and bookmarks, the daily wird, tasbih counts, the zakat sheet and, if you use For sisters, the excused days. It never holds your current location or your Google password.
  • On each device you sign in on: the account’s email address and the sign-in permission (kept by Google Play services on Android, in the Keychain on Mac, iPhone and iPad, and with Windows’ data protection for your user account on Windows).
  • Nowhere else. Sync uses no Salah Bar server. The file goes straight from your device to your own Drive over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. The developer never receives, sees or keeps the file, your email address or your sign-in.

How it is used

Only to sync Salah Bar between your own devices: each device reads the file, merges in what your other devices changed, and writes back its own part. Salah Bar doesn’t use your Google data for advertising, doesn’t sell it, doesn’t use it to train AI or machine-learning models, doesn’t use it to judge creditworthiness, and no person reads it.

Sharing

Salah Bar doesn’t share or transfer your Google data to anyone. Google stores it in your own account, under Google’s privacy policy.

Deleting your data

  • Sign Out, in Salah Bar’s sync settings, takes this device’s data out of the file and stops syncing on this device.
  • Remove My Data from Drive deletes the file from your Drive, with every device’s data in it, and takes away Salah Bar’s access to your Google account.
  • At any time, outside the app: remove Salah Bar’s access at myaccount.google.com/connections, and delete its hidden data in Google Drive on the web → Settings → Manage apps → Salah Bar → Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • Uninstalling Salah Bar doesn’t delete the file in your Drive; use one of the ways above.

Limited Use

Salah Bar’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Adhan on Amazon Echo (Alexa)

This is the one part of Salah Bar that uses a server, and only if you turn it on (Settings → Smart Home → Amazon Echo → Link Alexa, on Mac, iPhone or Android). An Echo can’t take audio from your computer or phone, so Salah Bar’s Alexa skill and a small server run by Salah Bar on Cloudflare (Cloudflare Workers) start the adhan on your Echo. A small function on Amazon Web Services (EU (Ireland) and US East (N. Virginia); Alexa uses the one for your account’s region) only passes Alexa’s smart home messages to that server and keeps nothing.

What the app sends

  • The times of your adhans for the next year (for example “Maghrib, 25 November 2026 15:22:03 UTC”), for the city you chose for the Echos and the prayers you chose, and which of Salah Bar’s adhan recordings to play. A year, so the Echos keep playing while your phone is away. It sends them again when the times change.
  • If you turn on Also on Echo for a routine, or use Play on Echo: which surah or duas to play (the addresses of the recordings the app streams) and when, and your Quran reciter for voice requests.
  • Not your city, location, calculation method or any other setting. The server never calculates a prayer time; it only plays at the times the app sent.

What the times can reveal:

  • Roughly where you are. Prayer times depend on the place, so someone who read a year of them could estimate the area they were calculated for, though not your address. The server keeps no city or coordinates, and no one reads the times except as described below.
  • Excused days. If you use For sisters and chose no adhan while excused, the app leaves the adhans of the excused days it already knows out of what it sends, so the Echo stays quiet on those days. A gap of a few days in the sent times could hint at that to someone reading them. The app never sends a prediction or any other cycle data.

What the server keeps

A random link id and a secret only your app knows (stored as a one-way hash); the tokens Amazon gives the skill to reach your Alexa and the anonymous Alexa user id Amazon assigns to the skill; the upcoming times and the routines’ recordings; where an Echo paused the Quran or duas, to resume; for each Echo that played, a number, the name and prayers you chose for it in the app, and an anonymous key derived from Amazon’s device id; and, for the last 7 days, when each adhan was due and when each Echo started it, so the app can show you and the server can start earlier by your usual delay. No name, e-mail address, voice recording, location or Salah Bar account.

What Amazon gets

A virtual button, “Salah Bar adhan”, appears in your Alexa app as a doorbell, and Salah Bar presses it at each adhan time; your routine then opens the Salah Bar skill on the Echo, which plays the adhan. Amazon handles this under its own privacy notice. The adhan recordings are streamed from Salah Bar’s server; Quran recitations from mp3quran.net and duas from hisnmuslim.com, as in the apps. Cloudflare runs the server for Salah Bar and may keep its usual short technical logs (such as the IP addresses of requests) under its own policy.

How long, and deleting it

Upcoming times are replaced at each update and dropped once played; start times are kept 7 days. Unlink in Salah Bar, or disabling the Salah Bar skill in the Alexa app, deletes everything the server keeps about you at once. A link the app hasn’t updated for 400 days is deleted automatically, and a pairing never completed after 1 day. To stop people guessing pairing codes, the server counts wrong codes per (hashed) IP address for a few minutes.

Who can see it

Only the server, to play the adhan, and Salah Bar’s developer when looking into a fault. It is not sold, shared or used for advertising.

Children

Salah Bar has no accounts and asks no one, including children, for personal information. The developer receives no data from anyone, apart from the adhan times of a linked Amazon Echo (above); Amazon Kids profiles can’t use the Alexa skill.
