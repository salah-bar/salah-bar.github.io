Salah Bar for Android: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

In short: Salah Bar collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your settings, your location and your prayer log stay on your phone, unless you choose to sync them through your own Google Drive or a file in your own cloud drive (or open Nearby mosques, which asks OpenStreetMap for the mosques around you, or a map, which loads map images of that area). The developer never receives any of your data. The one exception, off until you turn it on, is the weather: then your location, rounded to about 10 km, goes to Open-Meteo (see below).

What stays on your phone

  • Your location. With your permission, Android tells Salah Bar your location, to calculate prayer times and the Qibla direction (an approximate location is enough; Nearby mosques uses your precise location, if you allow it, to tell which mosque is nearest). The calculation happens on your phone. The place name (such as “Manisa, TR”) and its time zone come from a list of cities built into the app, so your location is never sent anywhere to be named. Salah Bar keeps your last location on your phone so the times are right the next time it opens. Your location is never synced to a cloud drive.
  • Your watch. If you install Salah Bar on a Wear OS watch, your phone hands it your settings, the city on show (with its location) and the prayers you marked around today, so the watch can calculate the times for its tile and complications; prayers you mark on the watch go back to your phone’s prayer log. They go over Wear OS’s own phone-to-watch connection (Google Play services), which may carry them through Google’s servers when the watch is connected by Wi-Fi rather than Bluetooth. Nothing reaches the developer. On a watch with GPS, the watch’s Qibla can use the watch’s own location if you allow it on the watch: it’s used on the watch only, never sent to your phone or anywhere else, and left out of backups.
  • The camera (only if you use it). The Qibla screen’s Camera view shows what your rear camera sees with the Qibla drawn over it, and Settings → Iqama → Scan QR code reads your mosque’s QR code on the phone; the picture stays on screen and is never stored or sent.
  • Your settings, your saved cities, your prayer log, your Ramadan log, where you stopped in each surah, and any adhans and recitations you download.
  • Whether you’re busy. To keep the adhan quiet during a call, or when the phone is on silent or Do Not Disturb, Salah Bar checks at prayer time whether a call is in progress and how the ringer is set. It never listens to or records anything, and stores nothing about it.
  • Your calendar (only if you turn it on). In Settings → Calendar you can let Salah Bar read your calendar’s events, to warn you about meetings that overlap a prayer, and write prayer blocks into a calendar you choose. The events are read on your phone and never sent anywhere. Salah Bar only ever changes or deletes the prayer blocks it created. If the calendar you choose syncs to an account (such as Google Calendar), that account’s service receives the blocks, as with any event you add.
  • Do Not Disturb during prayer (only if you turn it on). In Settings → Reminders you can let Salah Bar turn on Do Not Disturb for a few minutes at each prayer you choose. Android asks you to allow Do Not Disturb access first. Salah Bar only turns on its own quiet time and puts the phone back as it was afterwards; it keeps nothing about it but the current quiet time.
  • For sisters (only if you choose Sister). The excused days you log (when they started and ended, and whether after childbirth), pregnancy and nursing if you set them, and the fasts you have to make up are kept on your phone, to tell which prayers are excused and to predict the next start. Notifications, widgets and the watch never mention them.
  • Health Connect (only if you turn it on). In Settings → For sisters you can link Salah Bar with Health Connect, after Android asks your permission. Salah Bar then reads the menstruation periods of the last 180 days recorded there, so the prayers of those days count as excused, and writes the excused days you finish logging as menstruation periods. This data is used only for that: it is never sent to the developer or anyone else, never used for advertising, and never sold. You can remove the permission at any time in Health Connect.
  • The Tasbih counter is kept on your phone; it isn’t synced.
  • Reminders are scheduled on your phone with Android’s alarms and shown as notifications on your phone.

Sync (only if you turn it on)

With Google Drive. In Settings → Sync and backup you can tap Sign in with Google. Salah Bar then asks Google for one permission only, to keep its own file in your Google Drive’s hidden app folder (the drive.appdata scope): it can’t see, open or change any of your other files in Drive. It keeps the same sync file described below there, Salah Bar sync.json, sending it straight from your phone to your own Google Drive over HTTPS; there is no Salah Bar server in between, and the developer never sees the file or your account. Signing in is handled by Google Play services, which keeps the permission; Salah Bar stores only your account’s email address on the phone, to show it and to sync in the background. Google’s privacy policy applies to your Google account and Drive. Sign out takes this phone’s data out of the file and stops syncing here; Remove my data from Drive deletes the file from your Drive (every device’s data in it) and takes Salah Bar’s access away. You can also remove Salah Bar’s access at any time from your Google Account (Security → Third-party apps), and Drive → Settings → Manage apps lets you delete its hidden data. Salah Bar’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: it only reads and writes its own sync file in your Drive, to sync your devices, and never uses it for anything else or passes it to anyone.

With a file. In Settings → Sync and backup you can instead choose a file in a cloud drive you already use (Google Drive, OneDrive, Nextcloud or any app that appears in Android’s file picker). Salah Bar then writes your settings, saved cities, prayer log, Ramadan log, excused days (For sisters), Khatma progress and Quran places to that file, with your phone’s name (as set in Android’s settings) so your other devices can show where the data came from, and reads what your other devices wrote there. The file goes only where you put it: Salah Bar has no server, and the developer never sees it. Your cloud drive’s own privacy policy applies to the file. “Stop syncing” takes this phone’s data out of the file. Export writes the same data to a file you choose, and Import reads one back.

Android’s backup

If backup is turned on in your phone’s settings, Android may include Salah Bar’s settings, logs and downloaded adhans in your phone’s backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps, so they come back on a new phone. Salah Bar leaves its Quran downloads, its sync link and its Health Connect link out of that backup. You can turn backup off in Android’s settings.

What goes over the internet

Salah Bar only connects to the services below, and only for the feature named. None of them receives your name, your settings or an account, and only OpenStreetMap and the Overpass API (for Nearby mosques), OpenFreeMap (for the maps) and Open-Meteo (for the weather, if you turn it on) receive a location, but like any web request each sees your phone’s IP address.

The weather (off until you turn it on, on each phone). The sky behind the next prayer and in the widgets is worked out on the phone from the prayer times. If you turn on Show the weather (Settings → Appearance), Salah Bar first says what it will send, then asks Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) for the current weather at the city on show rounded to 0.1° (about 10 km), at most every 30 minutes while the app is open (never in the background), with no account, key, cookie or identifier. The answer (clouds, rain, snow…) stays on the phone, is drawn by its widgets, and is left out of Android’s backup. Turning the switch off stops the requests and forgets the last answer. Open-Meteo’s terms and privacy policy apply to the request; its weather data is CC BY 4.0.

Automation webhook (Settings → Advanced → Automation) is off by default: when you turn it on, Salah Bar sends the prayer’s name, its time and iqama, the city and the Hijri date to the one address you choose, at prayer times and around the adhan. That address must be on your own network unless you tick “Allow internet addresses”.

Links you tap (such as Tanzil.net for the Quran text’s source) open in your browser. Directions to a mosque open in the maps app you choose on your phone; that app’s own privacy policy applies.

Children

Salah Bar collects no data from anyone, including children.
